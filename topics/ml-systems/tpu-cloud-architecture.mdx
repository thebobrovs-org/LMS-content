## The one-line summary

You've seen what a TPU *is* — the chip, the MXU, the pod. But you never rent a bare
chip. TPUs are delivered as **host + chip VM units** you consume through one of a few
**paths**, and you shape jobs as **single-, multi-, or sub-host**. This is the
zoom-out: where TPUs sit in Google Cloud, and how you actually reach them.

## The unit: a TPU VM

A <Term id="tpu vm">TPU VM</Term> is a **host VM with attached TPU chips**. You don't
get the silicon directly — you get the host, SSH in, run your JAX or PyTorch-XLA
program, and it dispatches the heavy compute to the chips (no per-operation network
hop). Everything below is just *how you get and orchestrate these host+chip units*.

## Three ways to consume them

The same TPU VM can be reached three ways, trading **control for convenience**. Pick a
path below and watch the stack shift between "you manage it" and "managed for you" —
and notice **storage** and **networking** are always there underneath:

<Simulation id="tpu-consumption-explorer" height={520} />

- **Direct TPU VM** — request the VM (`gcloud` / Queued Resources), SSH in, run your
  code. **Full control**, you manage everything. Best for research and custom stacks.
- **GKE** — package your job in a container; **GKE** provisions, scales, heals, and
  gang-schedules the TPU nodes (this is the XPK → Kueue path you'll meet in the next
  lesson). The orchestrated path for **production training and serving at scale**.
- **Vertex AI / Agent Platform** — hand Google a job spec; it runs **fully managed**
  training, tuning, and serving (Vertex is folding into the Gemini **Agent Platform**).
  The **least ops**, the least control.

No matter the path, underneath it's the same TPU VM architecture.

## Workload shape: single-, multi-, sub-host

A second axis is *how many* hosts and chips one job uses:

- **Single-host** — one TPU VM. Small slices fit on a single host. Simplest.
- **Multi-host** — a slice that **spans several TPU VMs**, their chips wired together by
  <Term id="ici">ICI</Term> inside one pod; the host *processes* rendezvous over the
  <Term id="dcn">DCN</Term>. Essential for large, distributed training.
- **Sub-host** — use only a **portion** of a host's chips (e.g. 1 of 4) — granular
  control and cost optimization for small jobs.

(Join several *slices* over the DCN and you're back to
<Term id="multislice">Multislice</Term> from the topology lesson.)

## The rest of the cloud stack

The TPU VM is the centerpiece, but it never runs alone — a few Google Cloud pieces are
**always** in the picture (we go deep on these later; here's the map):

- **Storage** feeds the chips. A slice with no fast disk just starves — input pipelines
  read from **GCS**, **Hyperdisk ML**, Filestore, or Managed Lustre.
- **Networking** — the **VPC / <Term id="dcn">DCN</Term>** carries host-to-host traffic
  for multi-host jobs and checkpointing (the firewall/MTU detail is a next-lesson trap).
- **IAM, monitoring, logging** wrap all of it.

## Why this matters

This is the map the *mechanics* hang on. The next lesson — provisioning — is all about
**getting** these host+chip units (XPK → Kueue → DWS) and **paying** for them (the
capacity modes). It only makes sense once you see the landscape: a TPU VM as the unit,
a path to consume it, a shape for the job, and the storage + network around it.
