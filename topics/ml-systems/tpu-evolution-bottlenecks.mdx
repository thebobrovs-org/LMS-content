## The one-line summary

TPU history isn't trivia — it's the story of **chasing the bottleneck**. Every
generation introduced a feature *specifically to fix whatever broke the previous
one at scale*. The chain runs **compute → memory → thermal → network → workload**.
Learn the chain and the modern Pod stops looking arbitrary.

## Step through it

Click each generation and read the wall it hit and the fix it shipped:

<Simulation id="tpu-evolution-timeline" height={420} />

## V1 → V2: from inference to training

**V1** was a pure *internal* inference accelerator — a matmul ASIC to make Google's
own models cheap to serve. **V2** moved to **training** by adding distributed shared
memory, creating the first multi-chip **Pods**. V2 sets the **baseline (1×/chip,
1×/pod)** that every later jump is measured against.

## V3 → V4: heat, then the network

As performance density climbed, **air cooling failed** → **V3 introduced
<Term id="liquid cooling">liquid cooling</Term>** (≈3×/chip, 12×/pod). Then Pods grew
so large that **static copper wiring couldn't keep them reliable** → **V4 became
optically reconfigurable with <Term id="ocs">OCS</Term>**, reaching **≈6.6×/chip,
100×/pod**. OCS wasn't just for reliability — it was the **only way to physically
wire a 100×-pod** without the network failing, and it routes around dead chips.

## Megascale: v5p, Trillium, Ironwood

The naming tells the story: TPUs are `v{gen}{variant}` — **`e` = efficiency**,
**`p` = performance**. **v5p** (the performance variant) hit **21×/chip, 750×/pod**.
**<Term id="trillium">Trillium</Term> (v6e)** delivered ≈100× the performance of V2
at much better cost-efficiency. **<Term id="ironwood">Ironwood</Term> (v7 / TPU7x)** pushed
Pod scale to **9,216 chips** — it ships in two pod sizes (**256** and **9,216**), so
cite 9,216 only for the megascale config.

## The numbers, side by side

The "chasing the bottleneck" story is written straight into the spec sheet. Read
it left to right (oldest to newest) and watch every lever move at once — more
chips per pod, larger and faster HBM, wider interconnect, and steadily
**lower-precision math** (bf16 → FP8 → FP4) to wring more compute from each watt.
(Trillium = v6e, Ironwood = v7; the 8th gen splits into **8t** training and
**8i** inference.)

| Spec | v4 | v5e | v5p | v6e | v7 | 8t | 8i |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chips per pod | 4,096 | 256 | 8,960 | 256 | 9,216 | 9,600 | —† |
| Peak compute / chip (TFLOP/s) | 275 (bf16) | 197 (bf16) | 459 (bf16) | 918 (bf16) | 4,614 (FP8) | 12,600 (FP4) | 10,100 (FP4) |
| HBM / chip (GB) | 32 | 16 | 96 | 32 | 192 | 216 | 288 |
| HBM bandwidth (GB/s) | 1,228 | 820 | 2,765 | 1,640 | 7,370 | 6,528 | 8,600 |
| ICI BW / chip (GB/s, bi-dir.) | 600 | 400 | 1,200 | 800 | 1,200 | 2,400 | 2,400 |
| <Term id="sparsecore">SparseCore</Term> | Yes | No | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | No → CAE |

<Callout type="note">
"Peak compute" is each chip's **headline precision**, and that precision keeps
falling — **bf16 → FP8 → FP4** — which is itself a way of chasing the bottleneck.
For reference, v5p also reaches **918 Int8** and Trillium **1,836 Int8**. **†** TPU 8i
runs the **Boardfly** topology, so its per-pod chip count isn't quoted like the
torus pods (≈1,150 chips by HBM-per-pod math). Sources: Google Cloud TPU docs and
the Ironwood and 8th-generation announcements.
</Callout>

## The 8th generation: the workload split

By the 8th gen, the designers hit a new kind of wall — **training and serving
bottlenecks had diverged too far for a single chip.** Pre-training is bound by
**embedding lookups** (random-access token-ID → vector table reads — the *gather* that
<Term id="sparsecore">SparseCores</Term> exist to accelerate) and raw throughput; serving
is bound by <Term id="kv cache">KV-cache memory</Term> (the per-request attention buffer
that grows with sequence length) and low-latency collective aggregation. So the silicon **split**, and the naming
switched from `e`/`p` to **`t`/`i`**:

- **TPU 8t** — training (SparseCore, 3D torus).
- **TPU 8i** — inference (CAE replacing the SparseCores, Boardfly network).

That split is the loudest proof of the whole path's thesis: **the box is tailored
to the workload.**

## Why this matters

Every feature you'll use in the rest of this path — liquid-cooled racks,
OCS-reconfigured slices, the 8t/8i choice — exists because *something broke at
scale*. Knowing which bottleneck each one solved turns a spec sheet into a
predictive model: when you hit a new wall, you'll know what kind of fix to look for.
